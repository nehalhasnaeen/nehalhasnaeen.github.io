About Me

I am a machine learning researcher trained in electrical and computer engineering. I took an MS in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Idaho State University in 2023 and a BS in Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology in 2019. From August 2023 to February 2026 I held a graduate research assistantship at the University of Tennessee Knoxville, working on computer vision, multimodal deep learning, reinforcement learning and socially assistive robotics.

Most of my work has used data recorded from instruments. For my master's thesis I recorded frequency domain spectra from 1,000 passive UHF RFID tags and developed two feature reduction methods for them. A random forest classifier trained on those features separated tags carrying a SQL injection payload from tags carrying ordinary inventory data at 82.50% test accuracy, without reading tag memory. On FujiView, published at IEEE/CVF WACV 2026, where I was a co-author, more than 113,000 webcam images from 42 cameras were paired with concurrent and forecasted weather. Late fusion of the two modalities reached about 0.89 accuracy for same day prediction and 0.84 one day ahead, with visual features carrying the short horizons and weather forecasts the longer ones.

I taught as a graduate teaching assistant at Idaho State University for two semesters, covering four courses and their laboratory sections, with office hours, one to one tutoring and grading. I supervised one undergraduate from the biomedical engineering department through her first literature survey, which was published in Frontiers in Digital Health in 2025 with her as first author and me as a co-author.

I have worked in three fields, power systems, radio frequency security and computer vision, and published peer reviewed work in each. I am comfortable entering a new application area and intend to keep doing that.
